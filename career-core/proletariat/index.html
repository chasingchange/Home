Career Navigator

The Proletariat

A simple guide to the career search: how long it takes, how many applications it takes, when to follow up, and when to let go.

Most of what makes a search feel broken is silence. Most of that silence is normal. This page gives you rough numbers so you can plan instead of reading every quiet inbox as a verdict.

How long it takes

StageUsual wait
Apply to first reply1–2 weeks (sometimes 3–4 at large companies)
Recruiter screen to next step3–7 days
Interview to decision5–10 days
Final round to offer1–2 weeks
One role, start to finish3–6 weeks
Whole search, first application to accepted offerAbout 3–6 months

Ranges come from job-search data and vendor benchmarks. They vary by field, seniority and company size.

Spray-and-pray doesn't work well. Applications per hire have roughly tripled since 2021, and the odds of getting an interview have fallen by about half over five years. More volume mostly adds silence. Tailored applications and referrals move the numbers far more than sending more.

How to follow up

  1. Day 0: Apply. If the posting says no follow-ups, don't follow up.
  2. Day 6–9: Send one short follow-up to the recruiter. Add one new proof point.
  3. Day 14: No reply usually means no. Move on.
  4. After an interview: Send a thank-you within 24 hours. If the interviewer gave no timeline, check in 5–7 business days later.
  5. Stop at two: Two follow-ups total is the limit for any one application.

Simple weekly plan

WhenWhat to do
Weeks 1–2Build a list of about 20 target roles. Make 2–3 tailored resume versions. Start reaching out to warm contacts.
Weeks 3–8Apply 5–10 times a week. Follow up on day 6–9. Close out silence at day 14. Keep a weekly tally.
Month 2Fill in the calculators above. Adjust your targeting if your interview rate is low.
Months 3–6The usual window for a first offer. Plan your money around this range.

When to let it go

  • No reply after 14 days: Treat it as a no.
  • Silence for 3 weeks after an interview: Send one short closing note, then stop chasing.
  • An offer deadline you can't meet: Ask once, in writing, for more time.
